**`maintain grain` now checks a join declared after the baseline.** Before, `join_orphans_increased` covered only joins the baseline already held, so a relationship declared since the snapshot, or re-declared on other columns, was never checked. A join that was already wrong when the baseline was pinned was never reported either, because its orphan fraction became the "before". The grain axis now reads the joins declared today, the same way it reads declared grains. These come from the project (`relationships` tests, `references:` declarations, declared relationships) and from a native semantic layer (Ossie, Cube, SLayer); a dbt semantic layer's entity joins are not read here. A join the baseline measured under the bound is still compared with its earlier orphan fraction. Any other declared join is checked against a fixed bound and reported as `declared_join_orphans` at 20% orphaned or more (`high` from 50%). `data.baseline` (`absent`, `unmeasured` or `measured`) says why the bound applied, and a join that `join_orphans_increased` already reports is not reported twice. A declared join that does not resolve to the warehouse, or that names a missing column, is listed in `warnings`. `maintain check` runs the same probes. On a billed connector the new probes are priced in the same estimate, so a baseline mapped without `--verify` can now make `maintain grain` ask for confirmation, or `maintain check` make an offer, where it ran free before.
